## U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) – Reduce, Reuse, Recycle

**Category**

Federal Government

**Website**

https://www.epa.gov/recycle

**Description**

The primary federal recycling portal in the United States. This website serves as the foundation for most state and local recycling programs by providing guidance, educational resources, national initiatives, and links to specialized EPA publications.

**Information Structure**

- Recycling Basics
- Reduce, Reuse, Recycle
- Food Waste
- Composting
- Electronics
- Batteries
- Household Waste
- America Recycles Day
- Educational Resources
- Grants and Funding
- Frequently Asked Questions

**Notable Features**

- Comprehensive federal guidance
- Educational materials for schools and communities
- National recycling initiatives
- Gateway to additional EPA recycling resources

## Earth911

**Category**

National Recycling Information Service

**Website**

https://search.earth911.com

**Description**

One of the most comprehensive recycling search services in the United States.

**Information Structure**

- Search by item
- Search by ZIP code
- Recycling locations
- Material categories
- Educational articles

Users simply enter an item name (for example, "toaster", "paint", or "coffee maker") together with a ZIP code to locate nearby recycling options.

**Notable Features**

- Item-based search
- ZIP code search
- Nationwide coverage
- Extensive database of accepted materials

# 7. Summary of the Entire Research

This guide brings together approximately 150 recycling-related resources representing nearly every major level of the recycling ecosystem in the United States.

The collection includes:

- Federal resources maintained by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
- National nonprofit organizations
- Official recycling websites for all 50 U.S. states
- Municipal recycling websites from major cities
- Specialized recycling programs covering batteries, electronics, paint, mattresses, textiles, pharmaceuticals, carpets, and other materials
- Commercial recycling operators
- Industry organizations

Across all of these resources, six primary information architectures consistently appear.

### 1. Item-Based Navigation

Users search for the name of an object rather than its material.

Examples include:

- pizza box
- coffee cup
- toaster
- garden hose

This approach is used by Seattle Public Utilities, Earth911, Recycle Coach, Austin Resource Recovery, and New York City.

---

### 2. Material-Based Organization

Information is grouped according to recyclable materials such as:

- Paper
- Cardboard
- Plastic
- Glass
- Metal
- Electronics
- Batteries

This remains the most common organizational model among federal and state government websites.

---

### 3. Collection Container Organization

Many municipalities organize recycling instructions according to collection containers rather than materials.

Typical examples include:

- Blue Bin
- Green Bin
- Black Bin

This model is widely used in California municipalities and several large U.S. cities.

---

### 4. Geographic Navigation

Many resources begin by identifying the user's location through:

- ZIP code
- Street address
- County
- City

The user is then presented with recycling rules specific to that community.

---

### 5. Accepted / Not Accepted Lists

Nearly every website includes simple lists identifying:

- Accepted materials
- Non-accepted materials

### 6. Preparation Rules

Almost every successful recycling website emphasizes preparation before collection.

The most common guidance includes:

- Empty containers
- Clean containers
- Dry materials
- Flatten cardboard
- Do not place recyclables inside plastic bags

These preparation rules are consistently reinforced through photographs, illustrations, and educational campaigns.

---

Taken together, these recurring design patterns form the foundation of recycling education and public information throughout the United States. Although individual states and municipalities differ in their regulations and accepted materials, the overall user experience is remarkably consistent.

The most successful recycling websites combine clear navigation, simple language, visual examples, practical preparation instructions, and localized information, enabling residents to quickly determine how to recycle or properly dispose of almost any household item.
